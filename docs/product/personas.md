# Personas: planner and partner

**Status:** Draft. These are archetypes written from the product brief, not research. Sarah needs to confirm that the planner persona reflects her real experience (HUSH-30). Details marked "to confirm" are guesses.

In most couples one person ends up carrying the invisible work in their head, and the other helps when asked. HUSH is built for both roles. The two people may swap roles for different kinds of task.

## The planner

The person who notices when something is due and keeps track of it.

**Goals**
- Stop being the only one who remembers.
- Hand a task to the partner and trust it will get done without a follow-up.
- See what is coming up this month at a glance.

**Frustrations**
- Having to ask for help, which feels like nagging.
- Finding out a task was missed after it cost money or caused a problem.
- Reminders that feel like one more chore to manage.

**Current workarounds** (to confirm)
- Phone reminders, calendar entries, or sticky notes.
- Remembering dates by heart and reminding the partner out loud.
- Searching old emails or receipts to work out when something was last done.

**A typical month with HUSH**
- Adds new tasks as they come up, and checks the upcoming list once or twice a week.
- Assigns a few tasks to the partner, such as changing a filter.
- Marks things done when completed and relies on the due date moving forward by itself.
- Sees fewer "we forgot" moments and fewer reminders spoken out loud.

## The partner

The person who will do the task once they know about it, but does not track the dates.

**Goals**
- Know quickly what needs doing, why, and by when.
- Get credit for what they did without having to report it.
- Not be nagged.

**Frustrations**
- Being asked at a bad moment, with no context.
- Notifications that need opening an app and hunting for the detail.
- Feeling blamed for something they were never told about.

**Current workarounds** (to confirm)
- Waiting to be told, or acting only when the planner asks.
- Texts from the planner, which get buried.

**The "five seconds from the lock screen" bar**

The partner must be able to read a reminder, understand it, and act on it in five seconds, without unlocking the phone or opening the app. The notification itself says what the task is, why it is due, and who it is for, and offers one-tap actions such as Done and Snooze. If a reminder needs more than five seconds, it is too hard.

**A typical month with HUSH**
- Gets one or two reminders for tasks assigned to them, usually as a heads-up before the due date.
- Taps Done from the lock screen when finished, or snoozes it.
- Opens the app only occasionally, to see what is coming up.
- Is not asked about tasks that belong to the planner.
